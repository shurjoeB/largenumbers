Why Apollo? It is India's largest standalone hospital chain by market capitalisation — a number most readers intuitively grasp. At ~₹1 lakh crore, it is large enough to feel substantial, yet small enough that even mid-sized budget heads run into meaningful multiples, making the comparison easy to feel.

How to read these charts

Budget breakdown (top chart)

In × Apollo mode, each row shows Apollo Hospitals logos — one per ₹1,02,665 cr allocated. A partial logo shows the remainder. Hover a label for an explanation. Switch to Absolute ₹ for a proportional bar chart. The multiple label always appears to the right.

Historical trend (bottom chart)

Bars show the nominal or inflation-adjusted allocation for each year. The overlaid line tracks year-on-year percentage change — rising above zero means an increase, falling below means a cut. Dots are green for growth years, red for cuts. Select a budget head from the dropdown to explore it.

Inflation adjustment

Toggling to inflation-adjusted converts all amounts to 2025–26 prices using CPI (2011–12 = 100). This lets you compare real spending across years — for instance, a large nominal increase may be modest in real terms. The YoY % line recalculates accordingly.

Budget estimate vs actual

Older figures are Actuals; recent years show Budget Estimates (BE) or Revised Estimates (RE). BE is what was announced on Budget Day. Actuals sometimes differ. Each bar is labelled with its basis.

Data: Government of India Budget at a Glance ↗ and Expenditure Profile. 2022-23 and earlier are Actuals; 2023-24 is Actual (provisional); 2024-25 and 2025-26 are Revised Estimates (RE); 2026-27 is a Budget Estimate (BE). "Transfers to states" captures grants-in-aid only — excludes tax devolution (~₹1.4 L cr in 2025-26 RE). CPI deflator is approximate (base 2011–12 = 100); use RBI data ↗ or MOSPI for precision.
